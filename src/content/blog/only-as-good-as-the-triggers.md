Triggered emails are only as good as the triggers.

A well-timed message that lands because the customer just did something relevant is one of the most effective things CRM can do. An automated email firing off a rule nobody's reviewed since before your agents could write their own copy is just noise. Faster, better-formatted noise.

The difference is whether someone is actively thinking about the logic behind the flow, or just keeping the lights on while an AI writes increasingly polished subject lines for a broken trigger. We see a lot of the latter. Browse abandonment sequences that go to customers who already bought. Lapsed reactivation emails sent to people who never actually lapsed, just switched channels. Birthday emails on the wrong date, now personalized by an LLM that has no idea it's wrong.

The mechanics are usually fine—better than fine, honestly. The data hygiene and the logic review are where things slip. Nobody's agent is auditing whether the rule still makes sense; it's just executing it faster than a human ever could.

A proper CRM audit doesn't take long and tends to surface things teams are genuinely surprised by. We run them regularly for clients and the findings are almost always actionable within a few weeks.
